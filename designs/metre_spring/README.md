# Metre Spring

    N N4 U3 E4 U3 S4 U3 W4 U3 N4 N

33 blocks, 528mm of bore in 80 x 208 x 80mm. Five sections, 36 parts,
five cut files, one sheet each. 154 checks pass, with no warnings of any kind.

A square coil, four blocks a side, rising three between quarters. Designed to
be built on camera: five pieces, three of them near-identical middle coils, and
no plate longer than 82mm on its long side so the lot nests on one board.

Each file is `bore10-metre-spring-NNof05-<shape>-cut-files.svg`.

Mouth opens south at the bottom, bell opens north at the top, 256mm apart.
Sections 1 and 5 are the two ends and are mirror images of each other; 2, 3 and
4 are the coil.

## Why this shape

A metre buys about 33 blocks, and how they are spent decides everything. A
coil four wide rising three is the point where consecutive turns become
coplanar, so each quarter comes out as one bend rather than a pair of elbows
with a straight between. That is what makes it five files instead of eleven.

Rising less packs more turns into the metre but brings the coils close enough
to touch, which splits the pieces again. Rising more, or widening the coil,
spends the metre on height or width rather than turns and stops reading as a
coil at all.

The quarter count sets which way the ends face: an opening faces the entry
direction reversed but the exit direction as it stands, so five quarters points
them opposite and seven would put both on the same face.

## What it does not have

No elbows, so no single-block sections. No flattened plates and no tongues -
those only arise where a section meets an elbow, and there are none. Every seam
is a full four-sided coupling. Nothing touches anything, so every run of bore
has its own 3mm wall rather than sharing 6mm with a neighbour.
